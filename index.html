About

I am a cognitive neuroscientist studying how children learn to read and why some struggle. My work bridges neuroimaging, AI, and education policy to advance literacy for all students. I use precision fMRI to map the developing reading brain, build and evaluate AI-powered literacy tools, and translate research findings into actionable guidance for educators and policymakers.

I founded the Evidence-Based AI in Learning (EVAL) Initiative at Boston University to provide independent, rigorous evaluation of AI-enabled educational tools before they scale in schools.

Research

EVAL: Evidence-Based AI in Learning

AI in Education RCT Design EdTech Evaluation

I founded and direct the EVAL Initiative, an independent research collaborative that conducts rigorous evaluations of AI-enabled educational tools. Operating as an "FDA for Education," EVAL coordinates 10+ partner institutions across academia, industry, and policy to ensure AI tools demonstrate genuine learning impact before scaling in schools. Our current flagship project is a Bayesian-designed 3-arm RCT evaluating an AI reading tutor against human tutoring for 1st–2nd graders. GitHub

Conversational AI for Vocabulary Development and Educational Equity

AI NLP Vocabulary RCT

Vocabulary knowledge is the single best predictor of reading achievement and school completion. We developed and tested a speech-based AI avatar that delivers tailored vocabulary instruction in real-time, adapting to each child's progress via advanced NLP. A randomized controlled trial demonstrated effectiveness for vocabulary learning, with scaffolded instruction particularly benefiting struggling readers (d = 0.69–0.74).

Olson, H.* & Ozernov-Palchik, O.* et al. (In Press). Improvements on proximal vocabulary measures do not generalize to distal reading comprehension measures. Journal of Research on Educational Effectiveness. OSF

Ozernov-Palchik, O.*, Olson, H. A.* et al. (2022). Implementing remote developmental research: A case study of a randomized controlled trial. Frontiers in Psychology.

Neurocognitive Basis of Reading and Language Development

fMRI Neuroimaging Language Lateralization Dyslexia

Using precision fMRI on large-scale datasets, we demonstrated that the language-selective network is already left-lateralized by age 4 — challenging the long-held view that language is initially bilateral during early development. This work also investigates the neural substrates of reading comprehension in children with dyslexia, revealing distinct neural components underlying word decoding and higher-level comprehension processes.

Ozernov-Palchik, O.*, O'Brien, A.M.* et al. (In Press). Precision fMRI reveals that the language-selective network is established in early childhood. Developmental Science.

Ozernov-Palchik, O. et al. (2021). Distinct neural substrates of subcomponents of reading comprehension. NeuroImage.

Ozernov-Palchik, O., Sury, D. et al. (2023). Longitudinal changes in brain activation underlying reading fluency. Human Brain Mapping.

Learning Mechanisms in Literacy and Dyslexia

Dyslexia Speech Perception Statistical Learning MEG/EEG

Individuals with dyslexia exhibit speech-specific perceptual adaptation deficits and impaired neural representation of speech sounds, but these deficits do not extend to non-linguistic auditory processing. Despite these challenges, they achieve typical oral language — pointing to domain-specific learning mechanisms. This work uses psycholinguistic, procedural learning, and statistical learning paradigms combined with neural decoding approaches.

Ozernov-Palchik, O. et al. (2022). Speech-specific perceptual adaptation deficits in children and adults with dyslexia. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General.

Ozernov-Palchik, O.*, Qi, Z. et al. (2023). Procedural and statistical learning in children with and without developmental dyslexia. JSLHR.

Beach, S. D., Ozernov-Palchik, O. et al. (2022). Brain to text: Decoding spoken phrases from phone representations. Frontiers in Human Neuroscience.

Universal Literacy Screening and Early Identification

Policy Screening AI Prediction

A 50-state review revealed that universal literacy screening fails to connect students to intervention — a "leaky pipeline" from identification to instruction. We are developing AI-based approaches to scalable early reading prediction and investigating how technology can bridge this gap.

Ozernov-Palchik, O. et al. (2025). Universal literacy screening fails to connect students to intervention: A 50-state review. Scientific Studies of Reading.

Shangguan, Z. et al. (incl. Ozernov-Palchik, O.) (2024). Scalable early childhood reading performance prediction. AAAI 2024.
